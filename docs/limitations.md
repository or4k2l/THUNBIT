# Limitations

This document is an honest account of the known limitations and open problems
in the current THUNBIT prototype.  It is written to accompany — not to hedge —
the technical documentation.

---

## 1. False alerts on stable series

The most significant open problem.

Every synthetic stable demand series tested (across all seeds and all detector
variants) triggered at least one alert.  The stabilized variants (V4, V4.1,
V4.2, V4.3, V4.4) reduce the **number** of false-alert clusters and the **fraction**
of time spent in a non-STABLE state, but they do not eliminate false alerts
entirely.

This means that in an inventory or forecasting workflow, every SKU with stable
demand will still receive periodic "review" signals even when nothing has
changed.  The false-alert calibration problem is **unsolved** in this
prototype.

**V4.2 showed a major improvement** on this dimension: by normalizing the raw
confidence score against a rolling median baseline, stable-series alert
days dropped from ~32% (V4.1) to ~3%.  This confirmed that **score
calibration – not state-transition logic – is the central design issue**.
However, V4.2 also over-suppressed genuine breaks.

**V4.4 (V4.4b calibration) is the current recommended experimental tradeoff**:
it keeps V4.3's lower-quantile rolling baseline and warmup suppression, but
uses stricter state-entry calibration motivated by exploratory sampled-M5
checks.  V4.4 still fires false alerts on stable series; the improvement is
meaningful but the problem remains open.

The remaining challenge is that the raw confidence score carries a systematic
positive bias on stationary series because the KS statistic is sensitive to
finite-sample variation even when the underlying distribution is unchanged.
Scoring relative to a rolling baseline partially compensates for this, but the
right quantile, window, and threshold settings depend on demand volatility and
series length in ways that have not been fully characterised.

---

## 2. Only simulated data

All benchmark results in this repository are from synthetic demand series
generated by simple statistical models.  Real-world SKU demand typically
exhibits:

* Calendar effects (promotions, holidays, end-of-month patterns)
* External shocks not captured by AR or normal mixture models
* Autocorrelation structures that differ from the simulated scenarios
* Sparse or intermittent demand with structural zeros

Exploratory checks were run on sampled M5 retail item/store series to guide
follow-up calibration, but there is still **no formal real-data benchmark or
validation pipeline** in this repository.  Any observed performance on
simulated scenarios may not generalize.

---

## 3. Parameter sensitivity

The detector parameters (window lengths, thresholds, cooldown days) were
calibrated manually on a small set of simulated scenarios.  They have not
been validated through cross-validation, held-out test sets, or Bayesian
optimisation.  Different demand patterns will likely require different
parameter settings.

No automated parameter-tuning procedure is currently included in this
prototype.

---

## 4. Fixed window lengths

The reference window (`window_long = 90 days`) and current window
(`window_short = 21 days`) are fixed.  Shorter histories, seasonal products,
or products with strong trend may require different window lengths.  The
prototype does not adapt window sizes to the series length or properties.

---

## 5. No uncertainty quantification

The detector outputs a point confidence score, not a probability distribution
over states.  There is no credible interval, no posterior distribution, and
no mechanism for expressing "we have very little data here."

---

## 6. No multi-item or hierarchical support

The detector operates on a single univariate demand series.  There is no
mechanism for:

* Aggregating evidence across related SKUs
* Using category-level patterns to inform item-level detection
* Hierarchical change detection

---

## 7. Detection delay on some scenarios

The stabilized variants introduce detection delay relative to the baseline
detector, particularly on gradual-drift and intermittent demand.  V4 showed
a mean delay of 35 days on intermittent scenarios (vs. 12 days for the
baseline).  This is a direct consequence of the smoothing and confirmation
mechanisms.

---

## 8. Not a forecasting model

THUNBIT is a regime-change indicator.  It does not output demand forecasts,
safety-stock levels, reorder points, or any other operational planning
quantity.  It requires integration with a downstream forecasting or
inventory workflow to generate operational value.

---

## 9. Research prototype status

This repository is a research prototype.  It is **not** production-ready.
It has **not** been validated on real data.  It has **not** been peer-reviewed
or independently reproduced.  All results and claims should be treated as
preliminary and subject to revision.
